\section{Symbols}

\noindent Looking at profitability:

\begin{tabular}{ll}
  $\eta$    & overall profit-on-investment \\
  $i$       & capital bound into an investment (on day zero) \\
  $d$       & days an investment $i$ is bound  \\
  $p$       & profit for an investment $i$ after $d$ days \\
  $y$       & yield for a delivery
\end{tabular}

\noindent Looking at assets:

\begin{tabular}{ll}
  $e$    & earnings (also: fictional cost) \\
  $m$    & maintenance cost \\
  $l$    & track length
\end{tabular}


\section{Concepts}


\subsection{Profit-on-investment for a single investment}

The profit-on-investment per week $\eta_w$ is:

\begin{equation}
  \eta_w = \left(\frac{p}{i}+1\right)^{\left(7/d\right)}-1 \label{eq:weekly_profit}
\end{equation}


\subsection{One-shot line}
\label{sec:one_shot_line}

For a one-shot line, two terminal stations (between producer and consumer),
a track (of some cost and length), and a train is setup for a period of
$d$ days, and sold afterwards. The profit $p$ is the yield $y$ minus any
maintenance and deprecation costs.

A one-shot line is considered to have no shared assets and hence no associated cost splitting
and no profit splitting; all investments are made at day zero and are again liquidated at day $d$.

Overall investment and profit:

\begin{eqnarray}
  i &=& i\left(\mathrm{station1}\right)
     +  i\left(\mathrm{station2}\right)
     +  i\left(\mathrm{track}\right)
     +  i\left(\mathrm{train}\right) \\
  p &=& y
     -  m\left(\mathrm{train}\right)
\end{eqnarray}

(Since investment happens at day zero, the maintenance cost accumulated at day $d$ is deducted
from the profit at that day, instead of being added to the upfront investment.)

With these $i$ and $p$, the efficiency $\eta_w$ can be computed using eq.~\ref{eq:weekly_profit}.


\subsection{Shared assets}

When assets are shared then instead of calculating based on their investment, we calculate based on
some fictional cost $e$ for them. The fictional costs are the earnings $e$ contributed to each asset.

For the one-shot line from sec.~\ref{sec:one_shot_line}, this becomes:

\begin{eqnarray}
  i &=& i\left(\mathrm{train}\right) \\
  p &=& y \nonumber \\
    &-& \frac{d}{7} \cdot \biggl(
          e_w\left(\mathrm{station1}\right)
       +  e_w\left(\mathrm{station2}\right)
       +  e_w\left(\mathrm{track}\right)
       \biggr) \nonumber \\
    &-& m\left(\mathrm{train}\right)
\end{eqnarray}

There is no real value for the fictional costs $e_w\left(\mathrm{station}\right)$ and $e_w\left(\mathrm{track}\right)$,
we have to come up with an estimate that is overall consistent.

Obviously, longer tracks are less efficient, so earning should be split not by count $e_w\left(\mathrm{track}\right)$
but by miles $l \cdot e_w\left(\mathrm{mile}\right)$:

\begin{eqnarray}
  p &=& y \nonumber \\
    &-& \frac{d}{7} \cdot e_w\left(\mathrm{station1}\right) \nonumber \\
    &-& \frac{d}{7} \cdot e_w\left(\mathrm{station2}\right) \nonumber \\
    &-& \frac{d}{7} \cdot l \cdot e_w\left(\mathrm{mile}\right) \nonumber \\
  &-& m\left(\mathrm{train}\right)
\end{eqnarray}

Next, assets like stations and tracks are shared by more than one train, so the earning of each of these
should be fairly shared by the trains.
